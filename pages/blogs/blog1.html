Generative AI with Large Language Models – Part 1

An introduction to large language models, transformer architecture, and how modern generative AI systems are built.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are a powerful class of AI systems capable of understanding and generating human-like text. In this article, we explore how LLMs work and the foundational concepts behind training, tuning, and deploying them.

In 2017, the paper “Attention Is All You Need” was published by Google and the University of Toronto, introducing the Transformer architecture. Read the paper here .

Transformers rely on an attention mechanism that enables models to understand relationships between all words in a sentence, rather than just neighboring ones.

Transformer Architecture

The Transformer consists of two main components:

  • Encoder
  • Decoder

The strength of this architecture comes from its self-attention mechanism. Each word attends to every other word in the sentence, allowing the model to capture context and semantic relationships effectively.

Tokenization & Embeddings

Before processing text, input is tokenized and converted into numerical representations. An embedding layer then maps tokens into high-dimensional vectors that capture meaning and context.

Multi-headed self-attention allows the model to learn different linguistic features simultaneously, improving overall language understanding.

Types of Transformer Models

  • Encoder-only: Used for classification tasks (e.g., BERT)
  • Decoder-only: Used for generative tasks (e.g., GPT)
  • Encoder–Decoder: Used for sequence-to-sequence tasks (e.g., T5)
